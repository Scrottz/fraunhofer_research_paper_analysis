> D. Su, K. Kong, Y. Lin, J. Jennings, B. Norick, M. Kliegl, M. Patwary, M. Shoeybi, and B. Catanzaro, ‘Nemotron-CC: Transforming Common Crawl into a Refined Long-Horizon Pretraining Dataset’, May 30, 2025, arXiv: arXiv:2412.02595. doi: 10.48550/arXiv.2412.02595.

## Research Goal

Enable state-of-the-art LLM pretraining over long token horizons (15T+) by developing methods that preserve data quantity without sacrificing quality. Current aggressive filtering removes 90% of data, creating a bottleneck for extended training runs.


## NEMOTRON-CC ABSTRACT

**Metrics:** MMLU = Massive Multitask Language Understanding; Score = percentage of correctly answered multiple-choice questions (0-100 scale)
- **Problem:** Aggressive filtering in FineWeb-Edu, DCLM removes ~90% of data → unsuitable for long-horizon training (15T tokens)
- **Solution:** Classifier ensembling + synthetic rephrasing + reduced heuristic filtering
- **Results:**
  - Short horizon (1T tokens, 8B):
    - High-quality subset: +5.6 pp MMLU vs. DCLM
  - Long horizon (15T tokens, 8B, 7.2T from Nemotron-CC):
    - vs. Llama 3.1 8B: +5.0 pp MMLU (70.3 vs. 65.3)
    - +3.1 pp ARC-Challenge
    - +0.5 pp average (10 tasks)
  - Data efficiency (1T tokens):
    - Full dataset: parity MMLU with DCLM, but 4× more unique real tokens

- **Composition (6.3T total):**
  - Deduplicated original tokens: 4.4T
  - Synthetic tokens: 1.9T


## INTRODUCTION

- Context: Internet crawl = largest source of unique tokens for LLMs
  - Two purposes: high-quality content + diversity

- Status quo (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM):
  - Use model-based filters for high-quality content
  - Boost benchmark accuracies
  - Cost: remove ~90% of data

- Problem - Long-horizon training bottleneck:
  - Recent models train on extended token horizons
    - Llama 3.1 (8B–405B): 15T tokens
    - Gemma 2 27B: 13T tokens
  - Aggressive pruning creates data scarcity for extended training
  - DCLM & FineWeb-Edu contain ~80% near-duplicates (1T and 0.2T unique tokens respectively)
  - Training many epochs on same data = diminishing returns (Muennighoff et al., 2024); better to train on more unique tokens

- Proposed solution: Better accuracy-quantity trade-off via:
  - Classifier ensembling
  - Synthetic data generation
  - Reduced heuristic filter reliance

- Contributions:
  - 6.3T token long-horizon dataset: 4.4T deduplicated original + 1.9T synthetic
    - Released under Common Crawl Terms of Use (CC contributor framework)
    - NeMo Curator library (Apache 2.0): open-source data curation toolkit with reference implementation
    - Quality classifier models released
  - Effectiveness proof vs. DCLM/FineWeb-Edu:
    - 1.1T high-quality subset: +5.6 pp MMLU
    - Full dataset: parity MMLU vs. DCLM, but 4× more unique real tokens
    - 8B @ 15T tokens: +5.0 pp MMLU vs. Llama 3.1 8B (70.3 vs. 65.3)
  - Ablations show:
    - Ensembling selects larger, more diverse high-quality tokens
    - Rephrasing reduces noise + produces fresh variants
    - Disabling heuristic filters boosts high-quality token yield

- Guiding principle: Shift from static heuristic pipeline → learned flywheel
  - Improved data → better LLMs → better synthetic data & quality classification (virtuous cycle)

## METHODS

- Overview: Three-component approach to build English Common Crawl pretraining dataset
  - HTML-to-text extraction & heuristic filters (token yield)
  - Model-based quality labeling pipeline
  - Synthetic data generation
- Full pipeline schematic: Figure 3 (Appendix A)

### 2.1 HTML-to-text Extractor & Filter

- Foundation: Text extraction from HTML is major source of pretraining data
- Reference standard: FineWeb-Edu classifier (model-based quality classifier, proven effective)
- Goal: Balance data quality against token yield, avoid removing good tokens via over-filtering

#### HTML-to-text Extraction

- Tested: Justext (Pomikálek, 2011) vs. Trafilatura (Barbaresi, 2021)
- Qualitative assessment: both same level
- Quantitative (13 Common Crawl snapshots, Table 1):
  - Justext yields more tokens overall
  - Justext high-quality tokens: +28.6% (by FineWeb-Edu standard: scores 3–5)
  - Strategy: Maximize absolute HQ token count, not percentage
    - Justext: 127B HQ tokens (7.0%)
    - Trafilatura: 104B HQ tokens (7.5%)
- Language filtering: pycld2 + FastText language identifier (threshold 0.3)
- Deduplication:
  - Global fuzzy deduplication
  - Exact substring deduplication across eighths of snapshots
  - Tools: NeMo Curator library + deduplicate-text-datasets library

#### Filtering

- Conventional heuristic pipeline (from Parmar et al., 2024):
  - Raffel et al. (2020) + Rae et al. (2021) heuristics
  - KenLM perplexity filter (trained on Wikipedia + books)
- Problem: Heuristic filters remove 18.1% of high-quality tokens (FineWeb-Edu standard)
- Proposal: Apply heuristic filters selectively
  - Do NOT apply to high-quality tokens (identified by model-based classifiers)
  - Apply only to low-quality splits
- Validation: Ablation studies (Section 3.3)


### Model-based Quality Labeling

- Problem: Existing classifiers (DCLM, FineWeb-Edu) have limited recall (~10% high-quality tokens)
  - Bottleneck for long-horizon training
  - Quality labels not necessarily aligned with LLM downstream task performance
- Solution: Ensemble-based quality labeling pipeline
  - Build three classifiers with different quality preferences
  - Ensemble scores across all documents
  - Split corpus into quality buckets
  - Regroup into 5 quality levels based on downstream task performance

#### Quality Classifier Training

- Two annotation versions: prompt Mistral 8x22B-instruct + Nemotron-340B-instruct
  - Score documents from FineWeb on educational value (0–5 scale)
- Fine-tune linear regression models on Snowflake-arctic-embed-m embeddings (text-to-vector conversion model)
  - Frozen embedding + encoder layers (weights unchanged during training, only linear layer updated)
  - 20 epochs, learning rate 0.0003
  - Checkpoint selected by highest F1 on validation set
- Third classifier: DCLM classifier (fastText-based, Li et al., 2024)
  - Trained on instruction-formatted data + ELI5 subreddit high-scoring posts (Explain-Like-I'm-5: community-curated, high-quality explanations)
  - Stronger performance identifying high-quality tokens vs. FineWeb-Edu
  - Offers different perspective for increased recall

#### Quality Scoring and Bucketing

- Each classifier predicts quality scores for all documents
- Round scores to integers 0–19 (20 equal-sized buckets, ~5% of documents per bucket)
  - Bucket 19 = top 5% highest quality
- Final quality score: ensemble via maximum operation across three classifiers
  - Document distribution across buckets skewed by ensemble operation

#### Quality Labeling

- Map fine-grained scores (0–19) to 5 downstream quality categories
- Method: continuous pretraining annealing
  - Setup: 50B token training on 70% pretrained 8B model
  - Metric: average performance across 9 tasks per bucket
  - Weighted mix: 66% standard baseline data + 34% evaluated bucket
    - (Tests each bucket's contribution; higher gain = higher quality category)
- Group 20 buckets into 5 quality categories based on performance

### Synthetic Data Generation

- Observation: Low-quality docs contain noise/errors; high-quality have good writing/formatting
- Strategy: Different approaches for low vs. high quality

#### Low-Quality Data

- Goal: Reduce noise/errors while preserving useful information (cut training compute)
- Method: Rephrasing using medium-sized LM (Maini et al., 2024)
  - NOT creating new knowledge (unlike textbook/story generation approaches)
  - Transform text to Wikipedia style (Prompt 5, Appendix H)
  - Reduces errors, redundancies, improves formatting
- Result: Enhanced parallel corpus, lower perplexity, better downstream performance

#### High-Quality Data

- Goal: Generate unique tokens, condense essential knowledge
  - Rationale: Repeated tokens show diminishing returns after 4 epochs (Muennighoff et al., 2024)
- Four synthesis prompts:
  - Diverse Question-Answer pairs: ask varied question types (yes/no, open-ended, multiple-choice) with correct answers
  - Distill: rewrite as concise, clear passage
  - Extract knowledge: rewrite only informative content, discard filler
  - Knowledge list: structure key information as organized list
- Requirements: Clear, concise responses preserving factual info + concrete details (numbers, etc.)

#### Processing

- Chunking: Split documents into segments (complete lines, token limit enforced)
  - Prevents over-simplified model outputs on long inputs
  - Discard over-length lines exceeding token limit
- Post-processing:
  - Remove incomplete results, markdown formatting (e.g., **bold**)
  - Strip prefixes ("Here is a paraphrased version:", "Paraphrased Text:")
  - Remove enclosing quotation marks
  - Filter under-length outputs (< 50 tokens)
  - Wikipedia results: concatenate passages from same original document
  - Diverse QA results: shuffle pairs, append to segment end

#### Scale & Implementation

- Model: Mistral NeMo 12B (instruct, FP8 inference)
  - top-p: 0.9, temperature: 0.5
- Synthesis (Table 3):
  - Low-quality (403.0B raw) → Wikipedia → 336.3B synthetic
  - High-quality (451.3B raw):
    - Wikipedia: 372.9B
    - Diverse QA Pairs: 499.5B
    - Distill: 157.6B
    - Extract Knowledge: 303.6B
    - Knowledge List: 203.2B
  - Total synthetic: 1.8T tokens
  - Medium-quality: not synthesized (resource constraints)
- Tools: TensorRT-LLM + NeMo-Skills


### Putting It All Together

- Combine all techniques above on 99 Common Crawl snapshots (CC-MAIN-2013-20 through CC-MAIN-2024-30)
- **Nemotron-CC dataset: 6.3T tokens**
  - 4.4T globally deduplicated original tokens
  - 1.9T synthetically derived tokens

- Comparison to baselines:
  - ~4× more unique tokens vs. FineWeb-Edu-2 and DCLM
  - Reason: DCLM/FineWeb used sharded approximate deduplication only, contain ~80% fuzzy duplicatep

- High-quality subset (for fairer short-horizon comparison):
  - Nemotron-CC-HQ: 1.1T tokens
  - Composition: highest-scoring real data + diverse QA pair synthetic variants only

## EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment Setup

#### Training Setup
- Framework: Megatron-LM library (open source)
- Model: 8B parameter transformer LLMs
- Hyperparameter details: Appendix D

#### Data Blend
- Default: 1T tokens training
- Composition:
  - 73% English Common Crawl data (variable across experiments)
  - 27% fixed specialized mix: code, papers, books, patents, Wikipedia (Adler et al., 2024)
- Comparison method: vary only CC portion, keep specialized mix constant

#### Evaluation Setup
- Framework: LM Evaluation Harness library (open source)
- Tasks (10 total, metrics in parentheses):
  - ARC-Easy / ARC-Challenge (normalized accuracy): Multiple-choice science questions; Easy vs. challenging difficulty levels
  - Hellaswag (normalized accuracy): Predict next sentence in video paragraph descriptions
  - Winogrande (accuracy): Pronoun resolution under ambiguous context
  - RACE (accuracy): Reading comprehension from exam passages
  - PIQA (normalized accuracy): Physical intuition / common-sense reasoning about physical interactions
  - Social IQA (accuracy): Understanding social situations and appropriate responses
  - Commonsense QA (accuracy): Multiple-choice commonsense reasoning questions
  - Openbook QA (normalized accuracy): Fact-based Q&A with reference text provided
  - MMLU (accuracy): Broad multitask language understanding (57 diverse subjects: science, humanities, social science, etc.)

### 3.3 Ablation Study

#### Extractor & Filter Comparison

- Setup: 4 × 8B-1T experiments, vary HTML-to-text extractor + heuristic filter application
- Results: Table 7

![Table 7: Ablation studies on extractor and filter. HQ means high-quality data judged by FineWeb-Edu classifier (score 3,4,5). HQ-unfiltered means filtering is applied only to LQ data. See Appendix G for more details.](doc/assets/2412.02595v2/table_7.png)

- Extractors (HTML-to-text conversion):
  - Justext vs. Trafilatura: Justext yields +57.4% more high-quality tokens overall
  - Both qualitatively similar, but Justext quantitatively superior

- Heuristic Filters (automatic rules: length checks, symbol-to-word ratio, KenLM perplexity):
  - Standard application removes 18.1% of HQ tokens (excessive)
  - Proposal: selective application (remove from HQ only, keep on LQ/Medium)

- Downstream impact:
  - Trafilatura filtered vs. Justext unfiltered: marginal differences
  - Justext unfiltered vs. Justext HQ-unfiltered (filters removed from HQ only): +2.0 pp MMLU
  - Conclusion: Preserving HQ tokens by skipping filters improves accuracy

- Decision: Remove heuristic filters from HQ tokens only (maximize HQ token yield without accuracy loss)

#### Classifiers Comparison

- Three-classifier ensemble:
  1. Custom-trained (Mistral 8x22B annotations, linear regression on Snowflake-arctic-embed-m)
  2. Custom-trained (Nemotron-340B annotations, linear regression on Snowflake-arctic-embed-m)
  3. DCLM classifier (fastText-based, trained on instruction data + ELI5 subreddit)
- Question: Does ensembling improve HQ document recall?

- Classifier overlap analysis (CC-MAIN-2021-21, Table 8):
  - All three predict HQ: 10%
  - FineWeb-Edu (custom variant 1) only: 35.4%
  - DCLM only: 54.4%
  - Insight: Different classifiers capture complementary quality signals

- Downstream performance (13 snapshots, Table 9):
  - HQ token percentage: 9% (single classifier) → 25% (ensemble of three classifiers)
  - Average task performance: on-par or slightly better vs. individual classifiers
  - Conclusion: Ensemble increases HQ token recall while maintaining language understanding performance

#### Evaluating Synthetic Data

- Setup: 4 × 8B-1T models, different data blends (Table 10)
  - LQ-Base: original low-quality docs only (baseline)
  - LQ-Synthetic: LQ docs rephrased to Wikipedia style (noise/error reduction, same token count)
  - HQ-Base: 8 epochs high-quality + lower proportion LQ/Medium (quality-heavy blend)
  - HQ-Synthetic: HQ-Base with 4/8 HQ epochs replaced by synthetic variants (QA, Distill, Extract Knowledge, Knowledge List)

- Low-quality rephrasing (LQ-Base vs. LQ-Synthetic):
  - Average gain: +1.50 pp
  - Strong gains: +1.80–4.75 pp on ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, OpenBook QA, CommonsenseQA
  - Minor drops on some tasks (potential synthesis artifacts); flagged for future refinement

- High-quality synthetic swap (HQ-Base vs. HQ-Synthetic):
  - Replacing 4/8 repeated HQ epochs with synthetic variants improves most benchmarks
  - Hypothesis: Fresh unique tokens + diverse styles improve learning of specific abilities (e.g., QA, knowledge absorption)

## RELATED WORK

- Phi series: Small high-quality data + synthetic, but short token horizons
- FineWeb-Edu & DCLM: Core comparison points; model-based filtering established
  - Nemotron-CC builds on this, improves filtering + data quantity

- Other CC datasets (C4, DOLMA, Gopher, RefinedWeb, TxT360): Extraction + non-learned heuristics
  - RefinedWeb (Penedo et al., 2023): 600B token web-only dataset
    - First to challenge multi-source curation paradigm
    - Aggressive fuzzy + exact deduplication (77% data removal)
    - Demonstrated web-only models competitive with curated corpora (Falcon models)
    - Limitation: High removal rates create bottleneck for long-horizon training
    - Nemotron-CC extends this: Reduces aggressive filtering, adds learned classifiers + synthetic augmentation
  - Other CC datasets: Similar extraction/heuristic emphasis
  - Nemotron-CC: Goes beyond with learned techniques instead

- Concurrent work (Zyda-2): Combines existing datasets (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM, Dolma-CC)
  - Nemotron-CC: Creates new dataset from scratch rather than combining

- Multilingual datasets: Out of scope; future work

- Synthetic data for pretraining:
  - Typical: Instruction pairs, stories, textbooks via GPT-3.5/4 (expensive LMs)
  - Key finding (Maini et al., 2024): Smaller models (Qwen-1.8B, Mistral-7B) adequate for rephrasing
  - Nemotron-CC: Adopts compact-model rephrasing, extends with specialized prompts for LQ vs. HQ

## CONCLUSION

- Main achievement: Improved accuracy-quantity trade-offs for long-horizon English CC pretraining
- Key techniques:
  - Ensembling model-based quality filters
  - Rephrasing low and high-quality documents
  - Reducing reliance on non-learned heuristics
- Public dataset: Split by quality level + type (real vs. synthetic variants)
- Enables future work: Quality vs. diversity experiments, short/long-horizon curricula design

## LIMITATIONS

- Model-based ensembling & bucketing:
  - Single strategy explored (resource constraints)
  - Possible improvements at high-quality end of spectrum

- Synthetic rephrasing:
  - No verification of factual accuracy/fidelity to originals
  - Risk of hallucinations or diversity loss (undocumented)
  - Only LQ + HQ rephrasing; Medium-quality excluded
  - Mitigation strategies needed for future work

- Pipeline ablations incomplete:
  - Language identification not thoroughly ablated
  - Likely room for improvement

- Language scope:
  - English only; multilingual extension deferred

- Decontamination:
  - Not performed (no consensus on best practice, impact uncertain)
  - Baselines (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM, Llama 3.1) also undecontaminated
  - DCLM analysis suggests contamination minor factor (MMLU increases post-decontamination)
  - Open question: Impact across different model sizes and token horizons (community research needed)
